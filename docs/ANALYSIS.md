At 15 m/s the turbine makes **8.1 W**, against a hotel load of 7.9 W. With a dead pack and a dead array it will hold the autopilot, GNSS and telemetry link alive the whole way down. This is the same argument that puts a RAT in an airliner, and on its own it justifies carrying the turbine.

## 8. Trade study — motor installation

| Option | MTOW (g) | C_D0 stowed | L/D | sink (m/s) | ΔC_D0 when running |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deployable dorsal pylon (baseline, as requested) | 1622 | 0.0247 | 17.6 | 0.540 | 0.00250 |
| Nose folding prop (Variant N) | 1551 | 0.0231 | 18.2 | 0.516 | 0.00030 |


Variant N is **71 g lighter**, has **4.5 % less sink** and no mechanism to jam, because it deletes the pylon, its actuator, its doors and the dorsal spine that swallows it. The dorsal pylon buys three things in exchange: the prop disc is clear of the ground on a belly landing, the nose stays free for a forward-looking sensor if the ventral turret is ever outgrown, and thrust acts above the drag line so power changes do not pitch the aircraft as hard.

The baseline keeps the pylon because it is what was asked for and the penalty is real but small. If endurance is the only thing that matters, build Variant N.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.7 % | 36.1 | +9.1 | 182× |

| 0.50 | 6.8 % | 98.3 | +71.3 | 1,425× |
| 1.00 | 13.6 % | 148.9 | +121.9 | 2,437× |
| 4.00 | 54.3 % | 353.3 | +326.3 | 6,527× |


**The sensor is not the limit — clutter is.** Sun-baked bare rock, dark soil and asphalt reach 55–70 °C on a summer afternoon, i.e. ΔT of +28 to +43 K. Anything below that threshold is indistinguishable from hot ground on a single frame, which puts the single-frame detection floor at roughly **0.5–1 m² of active flame**, not the 0.05 m² the raw NETD implies. Getting below that needs the contextual approach used by MODIS/VIIRS: compare each candidate pixel against the statistics of its own neighbourhood, require persistence across frames as the aircraft moves, and cross-check the visible camera for a smoke plume.
